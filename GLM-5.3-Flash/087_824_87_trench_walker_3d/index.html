LAYER ONE — THE FLOOR OF THE TRENCH

Your oxygen drains with every step, and the helmet lamp burns suit power — 2.5 units a second while it shines. Stand inside a glowing ring station — the Entry Bell, Coral Shelf or Riftmouth above, Ventside or Overlook in the abyss, Wreckside on the trench floor — to recharge. Ten species shelter in the trench: sweep your sonar to find them, then light one from close by to record it. Some love your lamp and vanish without it — others flee the light, and only a sonar outline will hold them still. The First Descent drops from the eastern floor to Layer Two, the abyss. The Final Spine, carved into the south wall beside the Overlook Station, winds 14 m further down to the trench floor — the darkest reach of all. Any ramp dropping 10 m or more demands a decompression stop on the way back up: hold still in the marked ring, or pay 15 oxygen. The Guppy runabout waits at its dock 15 m east of the bell — press G beside it and let it carry you between the layers, along the ramps only. At the bottom lies the wreck of the survey vessel The Meridian. Read her stern plate, if you can get close enough — she went down with all hands in 1972. Two species haunt the wreck — the Wreck Lamprey and the Mourning Jelly — and only the Guppy's own lamp will reveal them. Leave the sub's beam burning at the wreck (it drains the sub's battery, manned or not), then come within 6 m, aboard or on foot, to record them. Two currents sweep the open floor, and the Ash Chimney vent beside the crab's ledge lifts divers in its bubbles. When the survey is done, return to the Entry Bell — on foot or by sub — to file the expedition report.
