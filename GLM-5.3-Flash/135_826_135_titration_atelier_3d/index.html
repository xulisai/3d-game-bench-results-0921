The glass never lies: the liquid’s colour is the written linear-RGB formula, the etched marks are the fill, and the hydrometer’s depth is the heft — stocks weigh 1.00 / 1.20 / 0.85, blended by volume, banded to 0.05, then a fixed correction; a denser mix sinks the float visibly deeper. Reagent R reads one bit: a drop clouds the mix if and only if its heft has reached 1.10 — below, it stays clear. And pour order hides in the glass: red before cyan grows a sediment worth +0.02 heft; cyan-first reads clean; yellow never matters. Contracts judge three channels at once — hue within ±0.04 on the atelier dial, heft within ±0.015, and the reagent bit — miss any and the banner names it. Eight orders hang in fixed sequence: a pure colour, a dual match, a cloud-test, a layered pour, a hard three-tincture blend, and three rival samples to vet. Every contract carries a written ideal drip count: reagent drops, wasted batches and re-makes all tally against it — finish within +2 for three stars, +6 for two, +12 for one. Stars bank per contract, and correct accusations bank points. There is no ratio readout on this bench: your instruments are the hue, the hydrometer, the reagent, and your own arithmetic. The rival samples’ grey cutter is written and never varies (no hue, ρ 1.100) — measure, then accuse with the grey volume within ±0.02, or pass; a false charge pays a penalty. Tab — outside an active blend — opens the ledger: contracts × 150, correct accusations × 100, stars × 40, wastes × −30; S at 950, A at 800, B at 650, C below. R is the master reset.
